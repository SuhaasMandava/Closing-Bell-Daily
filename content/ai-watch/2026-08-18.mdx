AI-linked stocks sold off Tuesday as the 30-year Treasury yield climbed to its highest level since 2007, pressuring the high-duration growth names that have led this year's market. Semiconductor stocks bore the brunt of the move, with the Nasdaq Composite falling roughly 1.3% while a broad gauge of chip stocks dropped more sharply. Broadcom underperformed its peers after security researchers confirmed active exploitation of a critical vulnerability in its VMware vCenter software, adding a company-specific headwind on top of the rate-driven selling.

The move was concentrated in tech and AI names rather than a broad market rout — the equal-weighted S&P 500 was far less affected than the Nasdaq. The mechanism investors pointed to was straightforward: higher long-term yields discount future earnings more heavily, and they also raise the cost of financing the AI infrastructure buildout that underpins the sector's growth story.

## The number behind it

The 30-year Treasury yield's move to its highest level since 2007 was the catalyst, but the figure drawing the most scrutiny in Broadcom's case was a Bank of America estimate that the company's AI chip-financing vehicle could carry up to $370 billion in senior debt by mid-2029, with Broadcom disclosing as much as $29 billion of potential exposure under lease-backstop and residual-value guarantees tied to that structure. That estimate, first reported last week, continued to weigh on the stock alongside the VMware security issue.

## What to watch

Broadcom reports fiscal third-quarter earnings on September 2, which will give investors a cleaner read on whether the AI financing concerns are a valuation story or a fundamentals story. More immediately, several chip-adjacent names were lower ahead of a Tuesday-evening industry event, and this week's retailer earnings and Federal Reserve minutes could further shape the rate path that has been driving the AI trade's recent volatility.
