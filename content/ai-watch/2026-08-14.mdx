Chip stocks split sharply on Friday rather than moving as a bloc. AMD closed up 6.5% at $514.39 after pricing a $4.75 billion multi-tranche, investment-grade bond offering to fund AI infrastructure buildout, adding to a Baird price-target hike and continued buy-side rotation into the stock following its early-August earnings report. Broadcom moved the other way, giving back nearly 6% after a Bank of America note put a hard number on the debt load building inside its AI chip-financing vehicle. Nvidia, the group's bellwether, was essentially flat on very heavy volume. In after-hours trading, all three names were little changed: AMD ticked up to $515.80, Broadcom edged up to $393.55, and Nvidia slipped to $224.72.

AMD's bond deal drew strong demand and priced on favorable terms, and the market read the added leverage as fuel for data-center and AI growth rather than a balance-sheet concern — for now. Baird analyst Tristan Gerra separately doubled his price target to $1,250 from $625, the Street high, modeling AMD's AI GPU platform revenue reaching $147 billion by 2030. Broadcom's decline was driven primarily by the financing-vehicle note rather than any results or guidance from the company itself, though a separate report of an actively exploited security flaw in VMware's vCenter software — a Broadcom product — also weighed on shares. Either way, the move reads as a re-pricing of risk after the stock had climbed more than 13% year-to-date on custom-ASIC demand expectations.

## The number behind it

Bank of America analyst Tom Curcuruto estimated that Broadcom's chip-leasing vehicle — which raises capital to fund and lease custom AI accelerators to customers — could carry roughly $370 billion in senior debt by mid-2029 at 20-gigawatt scale, including about $150 billion of new issuance in 2027 alone. That figure is not debt sitting on Broadcom's own balance sheet: per the company's latest 10-Q, an investor partner assumed the underlying purchase and lease agreements, and Broadcom has agreed to backstop lease payments for five years, with maximum exposure capped at roughly $29 billion on the initial transaction. The note lands as investors more broadly scrutinize how much of the AI buildout is being financed through debt and off-balance-sheet structures rather than cash flow — a question AMD's own bond sale, on the same day, put directly in front of the market.

## What to watch

Broadcom reports fiscal third-quarter 2026 results in early September, which should offer the next real data points on custom AI accelerator revenue growth and any management commentary addressing the financing-vehicle scrutiny directly. For AMD, the bull case embedded in Baird's target rests on the Helios rack-scale platform and Instinct GPU volumes scaling through 2027 — progress that will show up incrementally in customer deployment announcements, and in how the market prices the new debt load, before it shows up in a quarterly print.
