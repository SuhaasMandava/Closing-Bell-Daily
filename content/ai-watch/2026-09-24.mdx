The AI trade split again. Oracle fell 3.47% to $139.54 after a report that the company was invoking force majeure to protect itself from liability if a data center under construction in New Mexico is delayed. Broadcom dropped 1.30% to $350.36 and Nvidia 0.41% to $224.58, while AMD gained 2.38% to $629.26 and Micron 0.81% to $1,080.53.

## What moved

| Name | Change | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ORCL | -3.47% | Force majeure report on New Mexico project |
| AVGO | -1.30% | Lagged peers |
| NVDA | -0.41% | Little changed |
| MU | +0.81% | Held above $1,000 |
| AMD | +2.38% | Best performer of the group |

The Oracle report matters because delays in construction can push back revenue for the chip and server suppliers that sell into the buildout. A force majeure clause is a legal protection for delays outside a company's control. Using one does not mean a project will be late, and the sources reviewed did not give a timeline.

## The number behind it

**5.22%.** The 10-year yield's intraday high was the highest since June 2007. Oracle has been among the most visible borrowers financing AI capacity, and it has now fallen 3.1% and 3.5% in back-to-back sessions as the yield climbed.

## What to watch

Oracle's next update on the New Mexico project, and the 10-year yield. Chip names have held up better than the borrowers, which suggests the market sees the bigger risk in financing than in demand.
