The Fed's rate hike did not break the AI trade. After an intraday drop that took the S&P 500 down about 1% from its afternoon level, the tech-heavy Nasdaq recovered to close flat, and the chip names ended higher. AMD gained 1.65% to $512.50, Nvidia added 0.82% to $213.90, and Broadcom edged up 0.07% to $339.51.

Intel was the strongest of the group, up 4.03% to $101.05. Oracle rose 2.00% and Microsoft fell 1.37%.

The rotation was unusual: higher yields normally hit long-duration tech first. Wednesday did the opposite, with the technology sector up 0.1% while financials, energy and materials sold off.

## The number behind it

**5.01%.** The 10-year yield ended above 5% after the Fed's decision and Warsh's remarks. It is the level the AI trade has had to contend with since mid-September, and it has not yet produced a sustained drop in chip stocks. That suggests investors are weighing demand signals more heavily than financing costs for now.

## What to watch

Thursday's jobs and housing data, and then whether the 5% yield level holds. The AI-pacing debate from Monday is also unresolved and could resurface on any weak day.
