Chip stocks were mixed Friday as the AI trade split between two competing storylines: Broadcom's push to line up fresh AI-chip financing, and lingering digestion of Marvell's Google warrant deal from earlier in the week. Nvidia slipped modestly ahead of its August 26 earnings report, while AMD and Broadcom edged higher.

## What moved

| Name | Change | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| AVGO | +1.30% | BMO initiated coverage at Outperform with a $455 target; reports surfaced of a possible ~$100B AI financing package with Apollo and Blackstone |
| NVDA | -0.28% | Stock drifted lower in a quiet session as traders position ahead of next week's fiscal Q2 report |
| MRVL (theme) | volatile | Shares gave back part of this week's gain as investors weighed dilution from the Google warrant against the deal's long-term revenue potential |

Broadcom was the standout of the group, extending a bounce off support near $360 after BMO's bullish initiation and fresh reporting on a large-scale financing effort to fund custom AI accelerator production for its hyperscaler customers. Nvidia, by contrast, traded in a tight range as investors held back from making big bets before Wednesday's numbers.

## The number behind it

The financing figure drawing attention is the roughly $100 billion package Broadcom is reportedly discussing with private-credit firms Apollo and Blackstone to fund custom AI chip production — an extension of an earlier arrangement in which the two firms helped raise $35 billion to purchase Broadcom accelerators for lease to Anthropic. Broadcom has separately guided to $16 billion in AI semiconductor revenue for the current quarter, up from $10.8 billion last quarter, underscoring the scale of capacity these financing vehicles are meant to support.

## What to watch

Nvidia reports fiscal second-quarter results after the close on August 26, with Wall Street looking for roughly $2.09 in EPS on continued Data Center strength. Marvell follows a day later on August 27, where investors will get more detail on how the Google warrant agreement — and its associated dilution — factors into forward guidance. Any further detail on the size or structure of Broadcom's financing talks with Apollo and Blackstone is also likely to move the stock in the sessions ahead.
