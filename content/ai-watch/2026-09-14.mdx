The weekend brought an unusual kind of headline for the AI trade: public warnings from the leaders of Anthropic, OpenAI and xAI about the risks of developing the technology so quickly. On Monday, investors treated it as a reason to question valuations. Nvidia fell 3.36% to $210.96, AMD lost 4.40% to $493.41, and Broadcom dropped 4.77% to $344.72.

Micron fell 5.25% and Intel 5.59%, so the selling reached memory and CPU names, not just the large accelerator vendors.

## What moved

| Name | Change | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| INTC | -5.59% | Worst of the large chip names |
| MU | -5.25% | Memory fell with the group |
| AVGO | -4.77% | Largest loss among the three core names |
| AMD | -4.40% | Gave back Friday's gain and more |
| NVDA | -3.36% | Smallest drop of the core group |
| MSFT | +1.97% | Rose against the tape |

The warnings did not include any change in orders, guidance or spending plans. The selloff was a repricing of how long the buildout can run at its current speed, with a 10-year yield that touched 5% making the repricing steeper.

## The number behind it

**$21.7 billion.** That is the AI semiconductor revenue Broadcom guided to for its fiscal fourth quarter when it reported on September 2. Monday's drop did not change the guide. Broadcom fell 4.8% against an unchanged forecast, which shows how much of the move was about sentiment rather than company data.

## What to watch

Whether the pacing debate produces anything concrete, such as policy proposals or changes to spending plans, will decide if Monday was a one-day move. Until then, Wednesday's Fed decision is the bigger driver for the group.
