AI-linked chip stocks fell Thursday as a fourth straight down day for the major indexes dragged the high-multiple corner of the market lower. Nvidia and AMD led declines, while Broadcom held up relatively better, as rising Treasury yields and oil prices above $100 a barrel pressured growth stocks broadly. The move looked more macro-driven than demand-driven, with no fresh AI-specific news matching the scale of the selloff.

The pattern looked less like a reassessment of AI spending and more like a rate-driven derating of the sector's richest multiples. NVDA, AMD, and AVGO all trade at premiums built on multi-year capex assumptions, which get more expensive to justify as long-term borrowing costs climb.

## The number behind it

The 10-year Treasury yield traded around 4.85% Thursday, its highest level since October 2023, according to Treasury market data. The move has been driven by a mix of factors: oil prices climbing past $100 a barrel amid the prolonged conflict between the U.S. and Iran, heavy corporate bond issuance — with AI-related companies alone accounting for more than $1.5 trillion of new debt this year — and building expectations that the Federal Reserve could raise rates at next week's meeting. Higher long-term yields compress the present value of future earnings, which hits capital-intensive, high-growth chip stocks disproportionately.

## What to watch

Oracle reports earnings after Thursday's close, and investors are treating the print as a real-time gut check on AI capital expenditure trends following recent hyperscaler spending commentary. Beyond that, Friday's consumer price index report and next week's Federal Reserve meeting are the next catalysts that could move the rate backdrop — and, by extension, the AI trade — in either direction.
