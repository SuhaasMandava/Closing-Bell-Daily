AI hardware stocks fell broadly on Wednesday as a global semiconductor selloff that began in South Korea overnight spread to U.S. trading. The PHLX Semiconductor Index dropped roughly 5.6%, with all 30 of its components in the red. Broadcom was the session's worst performer among the megacap chip names after a rival landed a customer win that threatens one of its core franchises, while Nvidia held up better than peers heading into next week's earnings.

The selloff traces back to Asia, where South Korea's KOSPI triggered a circuit-breaker halt after SK Hynix and Samsung Electronics fell roughly 8% and 7%, respectively, and Japan's Nikkei extended losses. That weakness carried into U.S. trading and compounded existing worries about how AI infrastructure spending is being financed as bond yields stay elevated.

## The number behind it

The specific catalyst for Broadcom's underperformance was Marvell's newly announced custom-chip agreement with Google, which investors read as a threat to Broadcom's long-term arrangement to develop custom AI accelerators for Google's next-generation AI racks through 2031. Marvell shares, up roughly 155% year to date heading into the session, jumped on the news. Broadcom shares are also still digesting a Bank of America estimate that the company's AI chip-financing vehicle could carry up to $370 billion in senior debt by mid-2029, even though that debt sits with the financing vehicle rather than Broadcom's own balance sheet.

## What to watch

Nvidia reports fiscal second-quarter earnings after the close on August 26, the next major catalyst for the AI trade. With chip stocks now several sessions into a broad-based pullback, investors will be watching whether Nvidia's results and guidance can stabilize sentiment or whether financing and valuation concerns continue to weigh on the group into the print.
