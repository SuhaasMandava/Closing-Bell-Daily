Nvidia was the biggest AI-trade story Monday, announcing a $1.5 billion investment in SoftBank-backed SB Energy and a guarantee of up to $105 billion in lease payments to back a massive new OpenAI data center in Ohio. But the news barely moved Nvidia shares, and the broader market closed lower as oil prices and Treasury yields rose on renewed U.S.-Iran tensions near the Strait of Hormuz. The S&P 500 fell 0.52% to 7,745.06, the Nasdaq Composite dropped 0.32% to 26,644.91, and the Dow lost 0.51% to 53,459.78.

The muted reaction in Nvidia shares is notable given the size of the headline number. Part of the explanation: reporting also framed the deal as a scaling-back of an earlier, broader $250 billion guarantee Nvidia had reportedly discussed for OpenAI's data-center buildout, so investors read it as a more contained, deal-specific commitment rather than an escalation of Nvidia's balance-sheet exposure. AMD's underperformance looks more like broad-market softness and some profit-taking than anything company-specific — the stock had jumped over 6% on Friday.

## The number behind it

Nvidia said it will invest $1.5 billion in SB Energy, a SoftBank Group subsidiary, and will provide credit support on land, power and shell-building costs to secure an initial 4.25 gigawatts of AI-computing capacity — with an option to expand toward 8 gigawatts total — at the PORTS-Pike Technology Campus in Pike County, Ohio. OpenAI has signed a 20-year lease on the site, which multiple outlets reported could ultimately support as much as $105 billion in Nvidia-backed lease payments and cost up to $500 billion to build out in full. The arrangement follows Nvidia's broader push to directly finance the infrastructure that buys its chips: the company partnered with six major financial institutions on August 10 to launch platforms aimed at raising more than $500 billion in third-party capital for AI infrastructure, and Goldman Sachs has separately been courting banks, insurers and asset managers to participate in that initiative.

## What to watch

Nvidia reports fiscal second-quarter earnings on August 26 — the next real test of whether AI infrastructure demand and margin trends justify the financing commitments piling up around the sector. Broadcom follows with its own report on September 2. AMD is reportedly planning to raise up to $5 billion in its largest-ever investment-grade bond offering, adding to the wave of AI-linked debt issuance investors are watching for signs of overextension. This week also brings retail earnings from Walmart, Home Depot and Target that will shape the read on consumer spending, plus continued attention to oil prices and the Strait of Hormuz situation, which did more to move the broader market Monday than any single AI headline.
