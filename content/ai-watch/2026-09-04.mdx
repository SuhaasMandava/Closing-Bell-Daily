The Labor Department reported that U.S. employers added 162,000 jobs in August, well above the 65,000 economists had expected, with unemployment holding at 4.1%. The hotter print pushed September rate-hike odds above 60%, a backdrop that would typically pressure high-multiple growth names — but chip stocks moved the other way, with AMD and Intel leading a broad rally while Nvidia lagged with a modest gain.

## What moved

| Name | Change | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| AMD | +4.69% | Jumped to $477.57 as investors rotated into AI exposure beyond Nvidia |
| Intel | approx. +4.5% | Extended a run built on its foundry-turnaround thesis and 59% year-over-year growth in Data Center and AI revenue |
| Nvidia | +0.84% | Rose only modestly to $230.36, a one-session gap consistent with a broadening of the AI trade rather than a Nvidia-specific move |

The one-day divergence doesn't prove a lasting rotation out of Nvidia — hedge fund position data cited alongside the move predates the rally and can't confirm who was buying — but it's a clear signal that investors are willing to bid up the broader AI chip complex even against a hawkish rates backdrop.

## The number behind it

The August payrolls beat (162,000 vs. 65,000 expected) drove CME FedWatch odds of a September rate hike to roughly 60%, up from about 49% the prior session. Despite that, the iShares Semiconductor ETF (SOXX) outpaced the Nasdaq-100 by several percentage points on the day, with Nvidia's prior-quarter revenue growth of 106% and AMD's 50% cited by strategists as the underlying reason chip stocks kept climbing.

## What to watch

Whether the sector's resilience holds through the next CPI print and the September Federal Reserve meeting will say more about the durability of this move than any single session. On single names, Intel's ability to land a fresh external-foundry customer remains the swing factor for its turnaround thesis, while AMD's data-center ramp stays the key variable in whether it keeps closing the gap with Nvidia.
