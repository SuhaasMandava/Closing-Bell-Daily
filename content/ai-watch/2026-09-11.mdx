The AI trade rewarded the companies that sell the racks, not the chips inside them. Dell Technologies and Hewlett Packard Enterprise rose about 12% each, with Dell setting an all-time high, after Oracle's capex outline named server, cooling and networking vendors as recipients of its spending. Oracle itself slipped 1.7% to $150.28.

Chip stocks were mixed against a rising market. AMD gained 2.49% and Intel rose 2.61%. Nvidia ended flat and Broadcom edged up 0.32%.

The split showed where investors saw the next dollar of AI spending landing: on hardware that has to be bought, installed and cooled now, rather than on chip names that already carry high expectations.

## The number behind it

**$95 billion.** That is the capital expenditure plan reported from Oracle's results call, with its finance chief saying the spending would reach rack, cooling and networking suppliers and naming Dell and HPE specifically. Oracle is one of the largest buyers of AI infrastructure outside the biggest cloud providers, so a number that size is a direct read on orders for the hardware names.

The macro tape was kind too. August CPI matched forecasts, and the Nasdaq rose 0.96% to 26,333.04.

## What to watch

Monday is the first test of whether the server rally holds once the data-driven bounce fades. The Fed meeting on Wednesday matters for the whole group: a hawkish set of projections would hit long-duration AI names first.
