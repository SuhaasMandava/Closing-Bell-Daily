Chip stocks finished a choppy session little changed even as the broader market sold off, with the S&P 500 and Nasdaq Composite both down roughly 1% amid a jump in the VIX and rising Treasury yields. The AI trade's relative resilience came as reports solidified over the past two days that Nvidia has begun shipping H200 chips into mainland China for the first time, a development that partially offsets a market otherwise on edge ahead of Nvidia's fiscal second-quarter results next week.

Broadcom was the standout of the three, holding a modest gain even as the Nasdaq fell nearly 1% and the VIX jumped almost 8% on the day. Nvidia and AMD were essentially flat, suggesting investors are treating the China chip news as an incremental positive rather than a catalyst big enough to counter the broader risk-off tone in equities.

## The number behind it

The Financial Times reported this week that ByteDance and Tencent have each received about 10,000 Nvidia H200 processors in recent weeks — the first confirmed shipments of the chip into mainland China since export approvals were granted. That's roughly 13% of the up-to-75,000-100,000 units per buyer that Chinese firms are licensed to purchase, according to multiple reports citing the FT's sourcing. Nvidia has been sitting on an estimated 500,000 H200 chips earmarked for Chinese customers, and its second-quarter guidance had assumed zero China data-center revenue; some analysts have floated a total addressable opportunity as large as $30 billion if restrictions continue to ease. Beijing, notably, is the party currently limiting volumes — steering firms to route chips through Hong Kong rather than the mainland to protect domestic chipmakers like Huawei.

## What to watch

Nvidia reports fiscal second-quarter results after the close on Aug. 26, and investors will be watching closely for any change to management's China revenue assumptions given this week's shipment news. Beyond China, the macro backdrop — a spike in the VIX, rising long-end Treasury yields, and renewed chatter about the Fed holding rates rather than cutting further — remains the bigger swing factor for AI-linked valuations in the near term. Broadcom's own fiscal Q3 report, due Sept. 2, is the next major data point for the custom-silicon side of the trade.
