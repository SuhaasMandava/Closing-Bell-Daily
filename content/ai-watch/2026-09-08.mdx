AMD shares jumped nearly 6% on Tuesday after management pointed to a roughly $3 trillion total addressable AI market at Citi's 2026 Global TMT Conference, while Broadcom gained almost 3% as it presented at the Goldman Sachs Communicopia and Technology Conference. Nvidia was the outlier, slipping about 2% and taking a breather after three consecutive sessions of gains as broader markets came under pressure from rising oil prices and renewed Middle East tensions.

| Broadcom | +2.99% | Rose to $368.61 while presenting at the Goldman Sachs Communicopia and Technology Conference |
| Nvidia | -2.01% | Fell to $225.73, pulling back after three straight days of gains amid broader market weakness |

The split session extends a pattern that's shown up repeatedly over the past week: investors bidding up AMD, Intel, and Broadcom even on days when Nvidia is flat or lower, a sign the market is increasingly willing to price AI upside across the chip complex rather than concentrating it in a single name.

## The number behind it

AMD's $3 trillion AI-market figure, cited at the Citi conference, is a total-addressable-market projection rather than a near-term revenue guide, but it landed alongside continued investor enthusiasm for AMD's data-center ramp. On the other side of the ledger, Nvidia's pullback came despite no company-specific negative news — the move tracked broader risk-off pressure tied to oil prices and geopolitical tension rather than any AI-demand concern.

## What to watch

Broadcom's next hard data point is its fiscal fourth-quarter report on December 9. For AMD, the follow-through question is whether the AI TAM framing translates into upgraded near-term guidance at its next print. For Nvidia, the key is whether Tuesday's dip is simple profit-taking after a strong run or the start of a more sustained rotation into the rest of the AI chip trade.
