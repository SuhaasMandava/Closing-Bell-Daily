The AI trade split along company-specific lines Wednesday even as the broader market fell. AMD rallied after CFO Jean Hu lifted her estimate of the total addressable AI chip market to $3 trillion by 2030, naming TSMC as the company's permanent primary wafer supplier and pointing to a fast-scaling data center roadmap. Nvidia and Broadcom, by contrast, drifted lower with the rest of the market as oil prices approached $100 a barrel and long-dated Treasury yields rose, pressuring rate-sensitive tech names. The S&P 500 fell 0.48% and the Nasdaq Composite dropped 0.64% on the day.

AMD's gain stood out against a backdrop of macro pressure hitting nearly every other corner of the market, underscoring how much of the current AI trade is now being driven by company-specific guidance rather than sector-wide sentiment.

## The number behind it

AMD's CFO pointed to a $3 trillion AI chip market opportunity by 2030, up from prior company commentary, and reiterated a target of $70 billion in data center sales by 2027. Sell-side analysts responded by pushing fiscal 2027 EPS estimates for AMD higher, with the consensus climbing from roughly $12.96 to $15.45 across more than 30 upward revisions, even as the stock trades near 130 times trailing earnings.

## What to watch

Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang is scheduled for a fireside chat at the Goldman Sachs Communacopia + Technology Conference on September 10, a potential catalyst for the stock after three straight down sessions. Broadcom, meanwhile, has no major scheduled catalyst until its fiscal fourth-quarter report on December 9. Investors will also be watching whether rising oil prices and Treasury yields continue to pressure the broader tech complex, which could complicate the sector-specific optimism driving AMD's guidance-led rally.
