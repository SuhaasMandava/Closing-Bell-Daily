The streak ended. The S&P 500 rose 0.86% to 7,656.98, the Nasdaq added 0.96% to 26,333.04, and the Dow gained 509 points to 52,573.29. Nothing in the day's data was good news on inflation. It was simply not worse than expected, and after four straight declines that was enough.

Oil helped as much as the data did. Brent settled at $104.61, down $3.02, and WTI fell 2.4% to $100.05. Both pulled back from recent highs, which took some pressure off yields and off the outlook for energy-sensitive companies.

Advancers beat decliners by about 2.1 to 1 on the S&P 500, and 24 of the 30 Dow components rose. The VIX fell 11.2% to 15.84, giving back most of the week's increase.

## The number behind it

**0.4%.** That was the month-over-month rise in August consumer prices, matching the consensus estimate. Headline inflation held at 3.4% from a year earlier. Core CPI rose 0.3% on the month, a tenth above the 0.2% forecast, though the annual core rate stayed at 2.4%, in line with expectations.

The mix was not comfortable, but it held no large surprise. Traders kept their view that the Fed will raise rates at next week's meeting, with CME FedWatch odds near 90%. The 10-year Treasury yield ended at 4.97%, so the equity gain came without a drop in yields. Consumer sentiment was a soft spot: the preliminary University of Michigan reading fell to 47.8 from 51.7 in August.

For the week, the Dow lost 1.6%, the S&P 500 lost 0.8% and the Nasdaq lost 0.7%.

## What to watch

The Fed decides Wednesday. The market has priced a 25-basis-point hike, so the new information will be in the statement and the projections: how many more hikes policymakers expect this year. Oil is the other variable. Friday's drop followed a spike, and the supply picture in the Gulf has not changed.
