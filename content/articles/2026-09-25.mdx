The week ended on a firm note. The Dow rose 478.64 points, or 0.93%, to 51,828.62. The S&P 500 gained 0.51% to 7,743.41 and the Nasdaq 0.48% to 27,068.72. Eight of the 11 sectors rose, and 22 of the 30 Dow stocks advanced.

Two positives arrived together: lower oil and progress on trade with China.

## What moved

| Sector | Change | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Communication Services | +1.3% | Led the S&P 500 |
| Utilities | -1.0% | Hurt by yields |
| Materials | -1.2% | Weakest sector |

Microchip Technology rose 5.4%. The VIX fell 5.1% to 14.87, and volume was light at 14.9 billion shares against a 20-day average of 16.8 billion.

## The number behind it

**January 10, 2027.** That is the new expiration of the U.S.-China trade truce, extended by two months after President Trump called his meeting with Xi Jinping "very productive," according to Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent. WTI fell 2.3% to $92.41 and Brent 2.1% to $104.32 as hopes grew that the Strait of Hormuz would reopen.

Bond yields were the exception: the 10-year touched 5.23% intraday, its highest since June 2007, before closing at 5.18%. The final University of Michigan sentiment reading for September was 48.1, above the 47.8 consensus.

## What to watch

Next week's jobs report and the progress of Iran talks. The October Fed meeting is now priced as likely to bring a second hike, and the data will test that.
