The Nasdaq Composite jumped 2.26% to 27,122.09, its first record close since June 2. The S&P 500 gained 1.49% to 7,764.70, about 0.4% below its August 13 high, and the Dow rose 366 points to 52,048.83. Communication services and technology led, and seven of 11 sectors finished higher.

Two relief valves opened at once. Oil dropped to an 11-day low and the 10-year Treasury yield slipped back under 5%, closing at 4.96%.

## What moved

| Sector | Change | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Communication Services | +3.6% | Best sector of the day |
| Technology | +2.8% | Chipmakers led |

Intel surged 12% and AMD about 10%, with AMD's market value reaching $1 trillion. The VIX ended at 14.87. Advancers outnumbered decliners by 1.4 to 1 and volume reached 16.5 billion shares.

## The number behind it

**$95.78.** That is where WTI settled after a 4.5% drop, extending a decline that began after oil topped $100 last week. Weekend headlines were not calm. Houthis claimed missile and drone attacks on Saudi Arabia, and the U.S. warned citizens against travel to the Middle East. But President Trump also hinted at talks with Iran's president during this week's UN General Assembly, and the market chose to price the talks over the attacks.

No major economic data was released.

## What to watch

The UN General Assembly week will produce headlines on Iran, and a U.S.-China summit is scheduled for Thursday. The 10-year yield staying below 5% matters more for tech valuations than the record itself.
