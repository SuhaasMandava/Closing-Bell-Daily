Wednesday's calm didn't last. The S&P 500 fell 0.87% to 7,641.16, the Nasdaq Composite dropped 1% to 26,067.17, and the Dow tumbled 703.84 points, or 1.32%, to 52,759.21 — its worst session since July 29. Treasury yields, which had eased after the Treasury's buyback announcement a day earlier, climbed straight back above where they stood before that news, undercutting the rationale for Wednesday's bounce.

## The number behind it

$4 billion — the buyback ceiling the Treasury announced Wednesday — turned out not to be the last word. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent told CNBC the debt buyback operation could end up larger than that figure, but the promise wasn't enough to keep yields down: the 10-year climbed back above 4.70%, erasing Wednesday's drop, as investors weighed whether more buybacks might actually add to inflation pressure given already-ample dollar liquidity.

## What to watch

Walmart's slide is the headline read on the consumer: management pointed to $4 gasoline having a "psychological effect" on spending, even as the company beat on both lines. Contrast that with Target, whose turnaround narrative got a vote of confidence from raised guidance. Also watch AI-adjacent names after tepid results from OpenAI, and the ongoing Iran standoff, which is keeping oil and shipping-risk premiums elevated heading into Friday.
