Earnings season started with a thud for banks. The S&P 500 slipped 0.02% to 7,805.45, the Nasdaq lost 0.15% to 27,204.38 and the Dow fell 180 points, or 0.35%, to 51,670.10. Six of 11 sectors fell, and 15 Dow stocks rose and 15 fell.

Health care and financials led the decline.

## What moved

| Sector | Change | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Energy | +1.7% | Oil stayed firm |
| Consumer Staples | -0.3% | Defensives slipped |
| Financials | -0.4% | Bank results mixed |
| Health Care | -1.3% | J&J fell 5.7% |

Wells Fargo fell 3.1% after revenue of $21.87 billion missed the $22.15 billion estimate. Citigroup dropped 5.6% despite higher profit, as investment banking fees fell 13% from the prior quarter and credit-loss provisions rose. Johnson & Johnson beat on earnings but revenue of $25.43 billion barely topped expectations, and it fell 5.7%.

## The number behind it

**13%.** That was the sequential drop in Citi's investment banking fees, and it explains why a profit beat was not enough. Financials also faced a flattening yield curve and credit worries.

New York Fed President John Williams supported one more increase this year but saw no need for more. The VIX rose 0.4% to 15.27, and the 10-year yield fell to 5.22%.

## What to watch

The September CPI report on Wednesday. Core inflation has been running 0.3% a month, and the market will want the headline number below August's 0.4%.
