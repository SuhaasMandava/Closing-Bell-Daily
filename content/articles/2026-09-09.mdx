Stocks closed broadly lower on Wednesday as a sharp rise in oil prices, a jump in long-dated Treasury yields, and escalating geopolitical and trade tension combined to sour risk appetite. The S&P 500 fell 0.48% to 7,636.36, the Dow dropped 0.77% to 52,380.66, and the Nasdaq lost 0.64% to close at 26,253.34.

## The number behind it

**$96.69** — the closing price of October crude oil futures, up nearly 4% on the day and closing in on the psychologically important $100-a-barrel mark. Rising tension around Iran and the Strait of Hormuz, a key transit route for global oil and gas, drove the move, with a senior Iranian official warning Tehran was prepared to escalate if U.S. strikes on its territory continue. U.S. gasoline prices hit $4.22 a gallon, the highest ever recorded for this point in September, and diesel reached $5.94 nationally — a combination that threatens to push inflation higher just as the Fed weighs its next move.

## What to watch

Oil and yields are now the two levers most likely to decide the market's next move. A further leg higher in crude — especially any sign of actual supply disruption through the Strait of Hormuz — would intensify inflation worries and pressure the Fed to hold rates higher for longer. On the trade front, Canada's retaliatory tariffs on roughly 700 U.S. products took effect this week, and the White House has floated bans on Canadian dairy, alcohol, and motorcycles starting September 29 — a dispute investors are watching for signs it spreads to supply-chain-exposed sectors like autos and industrials.
