The hike everyone expected still moved the market. The Federal Reserve raised its benchmark rate by 25 basis points to a range of 3.75% to 4.00%, its first increase in more than three years, in a unanimous vote. The S&P 500 fell 0.45% to 7,551.81 and the Dow lost 631 points, or 1.2%, to 51,461.90. The Nasdaq ended essentially unchanged at 25,978.43.

The intraday path was uglier than the close. The S&P 500 traded near 7,600 for most of the afternoon, then dropped to about 7,524 during Chair Kevin Warsh's press conference before recovering part of that loss.

## What moved

| Sector | Change | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Technology | +0.1% | Only major sector to gain |
| Materials | -0.7% | Cyclicals sold off |
| Financials | -1.6% | Hit as yields moved higher |
| Energy | -3.0% | Oil fell 2.7% |

Eight of the 11 sectors ended lower. Exxon fell 3.5% and Occidental 6.6%. Volume was heavy at 18.4 billion shares against a 20-day average of 15.3 billion. The VIX rose 3% to 17.71.

## The number behind it

**16 of 18.** That is how many policymakers projected at least one more quarter-point hike by year-end. Warsh said the economy had strengthened since June, with the labor market near full employment, and that inflation remained the central problem, with recent readings showing no meaningful improvement.

The 10-year yield closed at 5.01%. Brent fell $2.92 to $105.83 after reports that Saudi Arabia was offering more crude to Asian refiners through ship-to-ship transfers near Oman.

## What to watch

Thursday brings weekly jobless claims and housing data, then the market turns to October's meeting. With the projections pointing to another hike, each inflation and jobs report now carries more weight.
