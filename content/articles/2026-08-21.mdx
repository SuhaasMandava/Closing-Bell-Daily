Wall Street ended a bruising week on a better note. The S&P 500 added 0.43% to 7,674.37, the Nasdaq Composite gained the same to close at 26,180.45, and the Dow jumped 517.80 points, or 0.98%, to 53,277.01. It wasn't enough to erase the week: all three indexes still finished lower over five sessions after Wednesday's brief bond-market relief evaporated Thursday, sending the Dow down more than 700 points on a Walmart earnings miss and a fresh leg up in oil prices.

## The number behind it

The 10-year Treasury yield, the thing that's been driving this whole week, held around 4.71% — essentially back to where it stood before Wednesday's Treasury announcement that it would at least double its buyback of long-dated debt. That relief rally faded fast once yields rebounded Thursday, and Friday's equity bounce came even as long rates stayed elevated near multi-decade highs. Gold kept climbing alongside the volatility, hitting a three-month high near $4,590 an ounce on a softer dollar.

## What to watch

Nvidia reports earnings Wednesday and will set the tone for the AI trade after a rough stretch for chip and hyperscaler names. Fed officials gather at Jackson Hole next week, and PCE inflation and revised payrolls data are both on deck — any of which could reignite the yield volatility that just interrupted this rally.
