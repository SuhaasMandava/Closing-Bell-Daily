Large-cap futures are pointing modestly lower heading into Monday, while small-cap futures are extending Friday's record close. The split looks like a continuation of Friday's story — soft consumer data pressuring the broad tape while breadth away from mega-caps stays firm — now layered with a full slate of regional economic data due before the bell.

The pattern mirrors Friday: money still favoring small caps and hard assets over mega-cap growth, with commodities getting an added lift from the Iran standoff rather than a change in the growth outlook.

## The number behind it

Monday's economic calendar is front-loaded. The NY Empire State Manufacturing Index (8:30 a.m. ET) is expected to fall to 10.2 from July's 15.6. The NAHB Housing Market Index (10:00 a.m. ET) is forecast at 35.0, roughly flat versus 34.0 last month. The Treasury's TIC report on foreign purchases of US securities lands at 4:00 p.m. ET, with June's estimate at $150 billion versus May's $232.7 billion — a number that matters given last week's climb in the 10-year yield to 4.69%. None of these are typically market-moving on their own, but a weak Empire State print so soon after Friday's retail sales miss would add to the case that August's data has turned softer than the market had been pricing in.

On the AI side, Friday's selloff in Broadcom and Applied Materials — down on questions about how AI infrastructure spending gets financed rather than whether it continues — is still the live storyline heading into the week. Nvidia's move on August 10 to form AI compute financing platforms with Apollo, BlackRock, Blackstone, Brookfield, Goldman Sachs and KKR, aimed at mobilizing over $500 billion in third-party capital, speaks directly to that same financing question and is likely to stay in focus until the next round of earnings.

## What to watch

Nvidia reports fiscal second-quarter results after the close on August 26, and it's shaping up as the key event for the AI trade this month — the company has already guided to roughly $91 billion in revenue, so the market's reaction will hinge more on the next-quarter outlook and any signs of AI capex digestion among hyperscalers than on the headline beat. Before then, Monday's Empire State and NAHB prints are the first test of whether Friday's soft data was a one-off or the start of a trend, and the Iran-related pressure on oil and shipping remains the wildcard that could push energy and inflation expectations either way.
