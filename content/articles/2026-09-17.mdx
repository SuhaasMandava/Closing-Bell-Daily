The market took back the Fed's damage in one day. The S&P 500 gained 85.95 points, or 1.14%, to 7,637.76. The Nasdaq jumped 1.69% to 26,418.30, and the Dow added 316 points to 51,778.04. Nine of 11 sectors rose, and advancers beat decliners by 2.4 to 1 on the NYSE.

Three things moved the same way at once: oil fell about 2%, the 10-year yield dropped to 4.95%, and labor data came in strong.

## What moved

| Sector | Change | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Technology | +2.2% | Lower yields helped growth stocks |
| Consumer Discretionary | +1.4% | Lower oil, lower yields |
| Utilities | +0.9% | Defensives gained too |
| Financials | -0.1% | Only major laggard |

The VIX fell 12.8% to 15.44. Volume reached 17.6 billion shares against a 20-day average of 15.4 billion. AMD jumped 6.4% and Intel 7.7%.

## The number behind it

**196,000.** Initial jobless claims for the week ended September 12 fell by 10,000 from a week earlier, and continuing claims declined to 1.73 million. Strong labor data usually raises rate fears. Here it was read as evidence that the economy could absorb the hike, and it arrived alongside lower yields instead of higher ones.

Housing starts for August came in at an annual rate of 1.275 million, and permits at 1.394 million.

## What to watch

Friday brings industrial production and the question of whether the 10-year can stay under 5%. Oil is the other test: the Gulf supply picture has not improved, and Thursday's drop in crude may not hold.
