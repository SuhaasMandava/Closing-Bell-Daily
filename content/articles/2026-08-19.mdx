Wall Street snapped a three-day slide on Wednesday after the Treasury Department gave bond markets a reason to relax. The S&P 500 added 0.21% to 7,707.98, the Nasdaq Composite rose 0.16% to 26,331.09, and the Dow gained 0.22% to close at 53,463.05. Gains were broad, with 14 S&P 500 names touching new 52-week highs, though the rally lost some steam into the close after the Dow had been up as much as 0.7% earlier in the session.

## The number behind it

$4 billion. That's the new ceiling the Treasury set for its buybacks of 10-, 20-, and 30-year debt, more than double the prior $2 billion cap. Longer-dated yields had been climbing toward multi-decade highs this week — the 30-year touched its highest level since 2007 — as investors weighed heavy government borrowing against a wave of AI-related corporate debt issuance. The bigger buyback pulled the 10-year down 5 basis points to 4.65% and the 30-year down roughly 9-10 basis points to about 5.19%, and equities followed the relief lower in yields rather than any new growth signal.

## What to watch

Fed minutes released Wednesday afternoon showed several FOMC members were open to further easing, which markets will keep testing against incoming data. Also on the radar: the Iran ceasefire that lapsed without a replacement, still keeping oil prices and Strait of Hormuz shipping risk elevated, and a fresh round of earnings — Analog Devices already out, with more chip and retail names still to report. Whether Wednesday's calm holds likely depends on if bond yields stay lower or resume their climb.
