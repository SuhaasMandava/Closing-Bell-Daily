Good earnings were not enough. Meta's third-quarter revenue rose 26% to $64.6 billion and earnings were $7.40 a share against $6.52 expected, but its free cash flow was negative $2.12 billion, the first outflow in its history, as it spent $34.91 billion on AI. The Nasdaq Composite fell 2.60% to 26,008.86, its worst day since early June, and the S&P 500 lost 1.21% to 7,663.43. The Dow edged up 0.02% to 52,867.98.

Meta also raised the low end of its 2026 capex range to $135 to $145 billion from $130 to $145 billion, and said it planned to issue nonvoting shares to fund AI spending without diluting Mark Zuckerberg's control. Press reports put the stock down about 7%.

## The number behind it

**-$2.12 billion.** That was Meta's free cash flow in the quarter, compared with a positive $10.63 billion a year earlier. It frames the AI spending question in plain terms: the biggest buyers of chips are now spending more than their operations generate.

Initial jobless claims fell to 194,000. Brent rose 4% and WTI 2.4% to $99.47 after Houthi strikes on Saudi oil infrastructure.

## What to watch

The Fed meeting next Wednesday, more hyperscaler results, and whether Friday's data and oil keep pressure on equities.
