Breadth returned. The S&P 500 gained 0.58% to 7,818.93, the Dow rose 253 points to 51,521.28, and the Nasdaq added 0.45% to 27,599.79, another record. Ten of the 11 sectors finished higher, and 21 of the 30 Dow components rose.

Oil was steady, easing worry about a supply shock. Brent finished up 1% at $101.62 and WTI up 0.9% at $90.26, as stronger Middle Eastern export volumes offset continuing Houthi attacks on Gulf supplies.

Ciena gained 13.9%, the best performer in the S&P 500. The VIX fell 3.3% to 15.01. The 10-year yield dropped 4.2 basis points to 5.27%.

## The number behind it

**$105.6 billion.** That was the U.S. goods and services trade deficit in August, wider than the $101.9 billion expected, and July was revised up to $92.8 billion. The jump reflected a surge in imports. Strong import demand signals resilient activity, which keeps inflation and the Fed in focus.

## What to watch

Wednesday's Fed minutes. A 5.27% 10-year yield is still high enough to compete with stocks, and the minutes could show whether policymakers see a need to push it higher.
