The S&P 500 closed above 7,800 for the first time, and the Russell 2000 notched its own record, after a tame July producer-price report took pressure off the Federal Reserve. Chipmakers Micron and SanDisk led the tape, while Cisco's post-earnings slide capped the Dow's gain at a fraction of the broader market's.

Tech didn't top the sector table, but it explains the index-level move: SanDisk gained 15% and Micron 5.6% on AI-storage demand, and the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index added 1.8% to a near one-month high. Breadth was real, not borrowed — advancers outnumbered decliners by roughly 1.8-to-1 on the NYSE, and the small-cap Russell 2000's own record close showed the rally wasn't confined to mega-caps.

## The number behind it

July producer prices were flat on the month, versus economists' forecast for a 0.2% rise, and core PPI matched expectations at 0.2%. On a yearly basis, headline PPI cooled to 4.7% from June's pace, below the 4.9% forecast. That's the second soft inflation print in two days, after Wednesday's CPI held at 3.4% annually. Fed-funds futures responded by cutting the odds of a 25-basis-point hike at September's meeting to roughly 35-40%, down from near 50-55% a day earlier — a reminder that this cycle's rate debate is still about whether the Fed hikes again, not when it cuts. The 10-year Treasury yield eased about 4 basis points to 4.65%.

Cisco was the session's other big storyline. Shares fell about 8.4% even after the company beat estimates and raised its outlook, as investors focused on gross-margin compression tied to its lower-margin AI networking hardware — a case of AI demand showing up in revenue but squeezing profitability. That drag, more than broad weakness, is why the price-weighted Dow rose just 0.13% while the S&P 500 and Nasdaq gained roughly five times as much.

## What to watch

Fed minutes from the July meeting are due next week, alongside a fresh batch of Dow-component earnings — both will test whether markets keep leaning toward a September hold. Weekly jobless claims have been drifting higher, a trend worth watching for signs of labor-market softening that could cut the other way on Fed expectations. On the AI side, Cisco's margin story raises the question of whether other networking and hardware suppliers report similar mix-shift pressure as they scale AI infrastructure sales — a theme likely to resurface in upcoming hyperscaler and enterprise-tech earnings.
