Stocks fell across the board Tuesday as a sharp climb in long-term bond yields collided with fresh geopolitical strain, sending the Nasdaq to its worst session in weeks and dragging semiconductor names down hard.

## The number behind it

The 30-year Treasury yield pushed above 5.3%, its highest level since 2007, while the 10-year approached 4.75%. Rising long-term borrowing costs — fueled by heavy AI-related debt issuance and mounting concern over government deficits — are making growth and tech stocks look expensive relative to safer fixed-income returns, which is why the rate-sensitive Nasdaq bore the brunt of the selling.

Oil added to the pressure. West Texas Intermediate crude climbed toward $84–85 a barrel and Brent neared $91 as the U.S. signaled a prolonged blockade on Iran's tanker exports and rhetoric between Washington and Tehran escalated further, with Iran shifting its posture from "defensive" to "fully offensive."

## What to watch

Wolfspeed reports earnings Wednesday amid investor concern over its margins, and Walmart, Home Depot, and Target results this week will offer a fresh read on the consumer after a soft July retail sales report. With bond yields still climbing and the Strait of Hormuz standoff unresolved, traders will be watching whether Wednesday's FOMC minutes offer any signal on the Fed's next move.
