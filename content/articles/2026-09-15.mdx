The bond market set the tone again. The S&P 500 slid 0.45% to 7,585.73, the Nasdaq fell 0.78% to 25,981.57, and the Dow lost 328 points to 52,093.11. Nine of the 11 sectors closed lower, and decliners beat advancers by 2.3 to 1 on the Nasdaq.

The 10-year yield reached 5.04% and the 30-year 5.40% intraday, both the highest since 2007. Traders were also pricing a 94.5% chance of a Fed hike on Wednesday.

Exxon rose 2.6% and Marathon Petroleum 3.6%. The VIX ended at 17.20, up 0.6%. The 10-year closed at 5.00%.

## The number behind it

**$108.75.** Brent crude climbed $3.07, or 2.9%, to that level, and WTI rose 4.4% to $105.83. Both reached their highest since May 19. The trigger was reports that crude loadings at Saudi Arabia's Yanbu port had been suspended and European shipments canceled. Yanbu has become the main outlet for Saudi crude since the Strait of Hormuz closed, so any disruption there removes barrels with no easy substitute.

The second pressure was debt. Rising rates raised concern about heavily indebted companies, especially those borrowing to fund AI buildouts, and that weighed on tech and discretionary shares.

## What to watch

The Fed decides Wednesday. Markets expect 25 basis points, so the focus is on whether policymakers signal more hikes. A 5% 10-year is already a headwind for equity valuations, and the market has little room left for a surprise.
