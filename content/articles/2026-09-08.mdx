Stocks fell Tuesday to start a holiday-shortened trading week (markets were closed Monday for Labor Day). The Dow Jones Industrial Average tumbled 628.18 points (1.18%) to 52,786.07 — its second straight losing session — while the S&P 500 dropped 0.58% to 7,673.52 and the Nasdaq Composite slipped 0.32% to 26,421.41. Rising oil prices, escalating hostilities in the Middle East, and an escalating U.S.-Canada trade dispute all weighed on sentiment.

## The number behind it

4.8% — the level the 10-year Treasury yield pushed above on Tuesday, its highest since October 2023, as traders looked ahead to this week's CPI and PPI reports for clues on the Fed's next move at its September 16-17 meeting.

## What to watch

Friday's CPI report, which one market strategist called one of September's most important events, plus Oracle earnings later this week and any further escalation in the Middle East or the U.S.-Canada trade spat.
