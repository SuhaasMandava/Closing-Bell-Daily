Stocks edged to another record close after July's CPI report matched expectations, easing worries that inflation would force the Fed's hand at its September meeting. The twist: unlike most of this year's inflation scares, the debate isn't whether the Fed cuts — it's whether it hikes, and traders were pricing in roughly a coin-flip chance of a September increase after the data.

## What moved

| Sector | Change | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Technology | +1.4% | AI infrastructure earnings beat big |
| Industrials | +0.7% | Broad participation beyond mega-caps |
| Energy | -0.5% | Crude slipped as rate fears eased |

Eight of the S&P 500's 11 sectors finished higher, and the Russell 2000 actually outpaced the Nasdaq — a small-cap, rate-sensitive index doesn't usually move in step with an AI-driven tech rally, and its participation is what kept the day from being a narrow, mega-cap-only move.

## The number behind it

July's Consumer Price Index rose 0.1% month over month, putting the annual rate at 3.4% — exactly matching consensus and down from June's 3.5%. Core CPI, which strips out food and energy, rose 0.2% on the month and 2.5% year over year, also in line. That's not a low inflation reading by historical standards, but after a summer of energy-driven price shocks tied to the Strait of Hormuz standoff, "in line" was enough. The 10-year Treasury yield was little changed at roughly 4.70%, and the 2-year was similarly steady, with traders still pricing roughly a coin-flip chance of a hike next month rather than a hold. Earnings added fuel: CoreWeave, Nebius, and Super Micro Computer each surged double digits on quarterly results and forward guidance that beat Street estimates.

## What to watch

Thursday brings the July Producer Price Index, the next test of whether wholesale costs are still passing through from this year's energy shock. A hot PPI print would revive the hike debate the market just started to shelve; a soft one would reinforce today's read that inflation is cooling despite elevated oil prices. Also worth tracking: whether the Russell 2000's participation holds up, since a reversion to pure mega-cap tech leadership would suggest today's breadth was more coincidence than confirmation.
