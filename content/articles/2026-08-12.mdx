Inflation came in below consensus this morning, and the market took it as
permission to rally. Breadth was the story: advancers outnumbered decliners by
better than four to one on the NYSE, and the equal-weight S&P outpaced the
cap-weighted index — which has not happened on an up day in three weeks.

## What moved

| Sector | Change | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Homebuilders | +3.9% | Best day since March |
| Regional banks | +3.1% | Closed at session highs |
| Semiconductors | +1.6% | Lagged the tape for once |
| Staples | +0.2% | The only sector under +0.5% |

Rate-sensitive corners did the heavy lifting. That is the tell — this was a
rates rally that equities borrowed, not an earnings rally.

## The number behind it

Core CPI printed +0.17% month over month against +0.2% expected. Small miss,
but it was the third consecutive month of deceleration in core services ex
housing, which is the series the Fed has said it cares about most.

The two-year yield fell 11bp. Fed funds futures moved from roughly a coin flip
on a September cut to about 80% priced.

## What to watch

Retail earnings begin in earnest next week. Today's tape implies a consumer
that is holding up; the guidance from the big-box names will test whether
that is true or just hopeful. A soft print there would make today look like
a rates story that equities oversold.
