The S&P 500 pulled back a day after its first close above 7,800, as a weak retail sales report and a sliding consumer sentiment reading gave the market its first real excuse to sell in weeks. The pullback was shallow and lopsided: the Russell 2000 closed at a fresh record even as large caps slipped, and the losses concentrated almost entirely in AI-linked chip and equipment names.

## What moved

| Sector | Change | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Small caps (Russell 2000) | +0.30% | record close, led by energy and materials names |
| Info Tech | -0.5% | Broadcom, Applied Materials weigh on the sector |
| Broadcom (AVGO) | -5.5% | AI capex financing questions overshadow guidance |

This wasn't a broad risk-off day. It was a rotation: capital left a handful of AI infrastructure names and moved into small caps and commodity-linked stocks, so the headline indexes fell even as market breadth away from tech stayed healthy.

## The number behind it

Retail sales fell 0.6% in July, the weakest print in over a year, against a consensus estimate for a 0.1% gain. The University of Michigan's preliminary August sentiment reading missed too, coming in at 51.0 versus a forecast of 55 and down from 55.2 in July. Together the two reports pushed September rate-hike odds down to about 24%, from above 60% in the first days of the month, with the probability the Fed holds now near 76%. Separately, Broadcom fell about 5.5% and Applied Materials about 5.2% even after AMAT beat estimates and raised its own guidance — investors focused less on whether AI infrastructure spending continues and more on how it gets financed. Sandisk was the exception, trading up around 7% through the session on continued AI-driven flash storage demand.

## What to watch

Reddit joins the S&P 500 before the open on August 18, replacing AvalonBay Communities, and will be an early read on whether growth names have regained their footing after today's rotation. The more important question is whether the Broadcom and Applied Materials selloff was a one-day repricing of AI-financing risk or the start of something broader — the next wave of hyperscaler earnings and capex guidance should clarify that. Crude oil's climb on escalating US pressure over Iran and the Strait of Hormuz has been the main support for energy's outperformance; any sign of de-escalation would remove that quickly.
