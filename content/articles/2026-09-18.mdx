It ended as a split session. The S&P 500 gained 0.17% to 7,650.50 and the Nasdaq rose 0.39% to 26,522.54, both lifted by chipmakers, while the Dow slipped 0.18% to 51,682.64 and the Russell 2000 lost 0.5% to 2,860.40.

The weekly scorecard was mixed too. The Dow fell 1.7%, its third straight weekly decline and worst week since March. The S&P 500 slipped about 0.1% for the week and the Nasdaq gained 0.7%.

## What moved

| Sector | Change | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Technology | +0.8% | Semiconductors rallied |
| Utilities | -1.4% | Worst sector as yields rose |

Micron gained 3.9% and Nvidia 1.3%. Volume was very heavy at roughly 25.3 billion shares against a 20-day average of 16.2 billion, typical of a quarterly options-expiration Friday. The VIX fell 4.1% to 14.81.

## The number behind it

**0.0%.** U.S. industrial production was unchanged in August, missing forecasts for a 0.3% rise. Manufacturing output fell 0.3% and capacity utilization stayed at 76.3%. The 10-year yield rose back to about 5.00%, and WTI settled at $100.30, down 1.6%.

Markets priced a 54% chance of another quarter-point hike at the Fed's October meeting, according to CME FedWatch.

## What to watch

Next week has fewer scheduled catalysts, so oil and the 10-year yield will set the direction. The 5% level on the 10-year has been the line the market keeps testing.
